A score is not feedback.

You finish a block at 62%. You see a red X, read one line, and move on. Six weeks later you make the same mistake, because nothing ever told you what was wrong with the answer you actually chose.

The wrong answer is the interesting part.

It is the exact point where your reasoning left the road. A bank that only explains the correct option is telling you what is true — not what you believed, and not why it was false.

So every case in Medchanic carries four written explanations, one for each distractor: the mechanism it breaks, and the one fact that rules it out. 7,288 of them across the bank, and no two are the same.
